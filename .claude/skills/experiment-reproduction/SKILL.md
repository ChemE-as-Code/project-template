# Experiment reproduction

## Purpose and when to use

A reproduction recomputes published results from the authors' raw data with core's methods
and reports the differences. It shows that the data, the method and the numbers in a paper
agree, and it gives a design case inputs whose origin can be checked. Use this skill when a
published measured series (an electrolyser's polarisation, Faradaic efficiencies, the water
dissociation efficiency of a bipolar membrane, kinetic rates) is to be reproduced, compared or
taken into a plant, and whenever such a series is added or changed.

Three things stay apart:

- **reproduction**: the raw data processed by core's method, compared with the published
  values under tolerances frozen beforehand;
- **validation**: a model's prediction compared with measurements it was not fitted to;
- **design use**: the measured series as an input of a plant (`performance: measured` in
  `process/cells.yaml`, skill `electrochemical-cell-modelling`).

A reproduction does not validate a model, and a design case does not reproduce a paper.

## Required inputs and missing inputs

- The data set: its persistent identifier (DOI, with the version), the file names and the
  sha256 of each downloaded file, its licence and the paper it belongs to. Without a pinned
  version and checksum, stop: a reproduction against moving data proves nothing.
- The method as the paper states it (equations, constants, which runs are pooled, which
  windows are averaged). Where the paper is silent, ask the user or the authors; record every
  choice you had to make, never fill a gap silently.
- For an electrochemical series: the active area, temperature, electrolyte and membrane; the
  voltage basis (cell, iR-corrected, electrode against a reference); per point the current or
  current density and the duration; per replicate the voltage or the chronopotentiometry
  series and its window, the outlet flow with its unit, the **standard conditions of the flow
  meter** (never assume 273.15 K/101.325 kPa: meters differ) and its correction factor, the
  mole fractions of the products, the titration volumes and the titration constants.
- The published values to compare with, each with its location in the paper (figure, table,
  supplementary file).

## Workflow and tools

1. Pin the data: download into a cache outside the repository, compute the sha256, record DOI,
   version, file names, checksums and licence next to the reproduction (a `source.yaml` or the
   measurement file's `source:`). Do not copy third-party data, figures or text into a
   repository unless the licence allows it, and then with the attribution it asks for
   (a NOTICE entry); prefer a download script that fetches and verifies the pinned files.
2. Freeze the tolerances **before** comparing: per compared quantity the tolerance and why
   (the published rounding, the spread of the replicates, the instrument). Commit them first;
   a tolerance changed after seeing the result is a new comparison, recorded as such.
3. Write the measurement file `experiments/electrochemistry/<id>.yaml` (schema:
   `cheme schema electrochemical-measurements`) from the raw data, generated by a script when
   the raw files are many (mark it GENERATED, with the command that made it): one point per
   current density, one replicate per run, the source file of each replicate in `source:`.
4. Process it: `cheme experiments electrochem experiments/electrochemistry/<id>.yaml
   [--json] [--plot figures/]`. It computes per replicate and point the Faradaic efficiency
   per product, partial current densities, the water dissociation efficiency from the
   titration, power, heat and the charge and carbon balances. Fix every error finding.
5. Compare the processed means with the published values under the frozen tolerances, in a
   table: quantity, point, reproduced (mean, sd, n), published, difference, tolerance,
   pass/fail. Report a failure as found; do not adjust the method to make it pass.
6. Where a published value cannot be reproduced, find out why (a transcription error, another
   pooling, another window, a correction the paper does not state) and record the evidence.
   Draft a note to the authors when the cause is in the publication.
7. For design use, link the same measurement file from the cell (skill
   `electrochemical-cell-modelling`); the comparison stays with the reproduction.

## Validation and failure handling

- `cheme.electrochem-input-missing`: a standard flow without standard conditions, fractions
  without a flow, a titration without its method block or duration: find the value in the
  paper or the instrument's manual, or ask; never assume it.
- `cheme.electrochem-fe-sum-high` (error): more charge in the products of one electrode than
  the current (above `max(1 + 3 sd, 1.05)`): a wrong flow unit, standard conditions,
  correction factor, fraction unit or a product's `electrode`.
- `cheme.electrochem-fe-sum-low` (warning): an unmeasured product (liquids) or lost gas.
- `cheme.electrochem-crossover-assumed`: a membrane cell without a measured water
  dissociation efficiency.
- `cheme.electrochem-carbon-balance`: carbon in and out differ by more than 5 %.
- `cheme.electrochem-single-replicate` (info): a point without a spread; say so in the
  comparison.
- A rerun from an empty download cache must give the same numbers; when it does not, the
  pin or the script is incomplete.

## Outputs and provenance

- The measurement file (provenance `given`, origin `measured`, `source:` with DOI and
  version, `licence:`), the processed report (`--json`, with the model id and version, the
  core version and the file's sha256), the figures, the frozen tolerances and the comparison.
- Every number in the comparison traces to a replicate and a raw file; every deviation from
  the paper's method is listed.

## Required human judgment

- Which runs belong to a point, and which are excluded (a failed run, a leak), with the reason.
- The tolerances, before the comparison.
- Whether a discrepancy is the paper's, the data's or the method's, and whether to contact the
  authors.
- Whether the series may be used for design beyond the conditions it was measured at (it may
  not be extrapolated; core refuses).
