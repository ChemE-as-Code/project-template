# Absorption and stripping

## Purpose and when to use

An absorber moves a component from gas into a liquid solvent; a stripper moves one from liquid
into a gas. Treat the absorber and its regenerator as one system: the leaner the returning
solvent, the smaller the absorber, so they are sized together, and an absorber with no
regeneration path is half a unit (Ray and Das, *Process Equipment and Plant Design*, Elsevier
2020, Ch 10).

## Required inputs and missing inputs

Solute and solvent, inlet and required outlet concentrations, pressure, temperature, whether the
solvent regenerates by heat or stripping gas, and what the solvent degrades into. Ask for what is
missing.

## Workflow and tools

Engineering guidance only; core has no calculation for this. An absorber or stripper can be given
a column model in `process/simulation.yaml` (see `separation-method-selection`, downstream
representation: predictive but untested here, without rate-based or reactive absorption).

The loop in order: rich solvent leaves the absorber, exchanges heat with lean solvent, is flashed
if it was at pressure (releasing co-absorbed hydrocarbon), is regenerated by heat or stripping
(reboiler, condenser, reflux drum, solute outlet), is cooled and returns. Also: make-up, because
solvent leaves with both outgoing streams, and filtration or reclaiming, because a loop
accumulates degradation products, salts and particulates.

Countercurrent contacting is normal; cocurrent is chosen for a fast irreversible reaction or to
avoid a large gas duct between two halves of a very tall tower.

Heat: absorption releases heat of solution, so the liquid warms toward the bottom exactly where
loading is highest. Where the duty is large a side cooler (liquid drawn out, cooled, returned)
is normal.

Rules of thumb (same source): economic solvent rate about 1.5 to 3 times the minimum; below about
10 mol % the system is dilute with a straight operating line; with an absorption factor below 1,
recovery approaches that factor asymptotically, so more recovery needs more or better solvent,
not a taller column (the stage calculation, not this, says if a column has enough stages); packed
bed depth quoted is the active bed, distributors add height and pressure drop.

Control: level on both bottoms, pressure on the absorber, solvent flow usually paced to feed gas,
reboiler duty on a temperature standing for lean loading, a treated-gas analyser for quality.

## Validation and failure handling

Questions for a design: no regeneration path or lean return; high-pressure rich line with no flash
drum; no lean/rich exchanger; no filtration or reclaiming on a loop that will degrade; no make-up;
no lean cooler.

## Outputs and provenance

State the regeneration method and the make-up and clean-up provisions, with the source of any
rule used. Record in `DESIGN.md`.

## Required human judgment

Solvent choice, regeneration method, and handling of toxic or flammable solutes need a person.
